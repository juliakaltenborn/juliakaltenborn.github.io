### Research Philosophy
To me, researching is a deeply human act. It emerges when we follow our curiosity - playfully, collaboratively, and carefully - question our own beliefs, and cross boundaries into other disciplines or into the unknown. As a scientist, I consider it my duty to question my own privileges and our complicity in extractive systems, particularly in contemporary AI research. In my computational work, I want to remain grounded in the physical systems, people, and practices through which data are produced. In practice, this means: going out into the field.

### Community
My research would not be possible without the scientific, field, activist, and local communities that have raised and nurtured me. I try to pass some of that support on by initiating scientific communities ([ML4Cryo](https://ml4cryo.github.io/)), co-organizing humanitarian initiatives ([AI Helps Ukraine Charity Conference](https://mila.quebec/en/news/ai-helps-ukraine-charity-conference-a-look-back-on-the-in-person-event-at-mila)), and creating interdisciplinary spaces where different ways of knowing can meet ([Polar Eclipse School](https://polar-eclipse-school.github.io/)).
