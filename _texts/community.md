For me, community comes into being when I share a purpose with others. When we gather around a purpose, it gives us strength and belonging, and gives us the capacity to act together. Community is reciprocal at heart for me: when we genuinely participate in it, it sustains us—with purpose, belonging, strength, and resources to keep participating—and does not deplete the people who sustain it. As such, I prefer serving the Commons rather than serving the Capital.


At the same time, I am part of a system from which I materially and professionally benefit significantly—a system I am deeply critical of. I do not have a clean resolution to that contradiction. I am trying to live within that tension by creating spaces for community, engaging in two-way dialogue, and using some of my access to make academic pathways more accessible to others.


# Mentorship and Access
If you come from a background that is underrepresented in academia, or have had less access to academic networks and their unwritten rules, please feel welcome to reach out. I am happy to share what I know and offer practical support with applications, motivation letters and statements, or navigating academic pathways.
